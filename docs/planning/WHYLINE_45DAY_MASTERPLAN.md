# WHYLINE — 45-Day Master Plan

> Rethinking everything with time as a resource instead of a constraint.
> Companion to `WHYLINE_PROPOSAL.md` (7-day version) and `TOP7_IDEAS.md` (stack-agnostic ideation).

Written 21 October 2026. Horizon: 45 days (to ~5 December). **Caveat up front:** the official rules file says submissions close 27 October; the hackathon banner says the event runs to 21 November. This plan is built so that a credible, complete submission exists at **day 7, day 21, day 31 and day 45**, whichever deadline turns out to be real.

## Part I — What 45 days changes

With seven days, the only honest plan was "one engine, a newsroom, three screens." With 45 days, five things that were impossible become the differentiators:

| Unlock | What it enables | Evidence it is real |
|---|---|---|
| **Train models on our own unlimited labelled data** | Learned anticipation (what happens in the next 4–8 s), vision detectors fine-tuned on rendered frames with perfect labels, a learned pass/xT model, a tactical-phase classifier that reads tracking | FootBots-style set-attention motion prediction in soccer ([ICIP 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.19852)); Ball Action Anticipation is a SoccerNet 2026 task ([results](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07320)) |
| **A second, realistic 3D world with pixels** | Google Research Football (gfootball) renders 3D frames, exposes every player and ball position, saves/restores engine state, and has pretrained 11v11 multi-agent RL policies from DB-Football. That gives synthetic *broadcast footage* with free ground truth, emergent (not scripted) play, and a credibility story no 2.5D canvas can match | gfootball repo archived Aug 2026 but pip/Docker installable ([repo](https://github.com/google-research/football)); DB-Football ships 5v5 and 11v11 trained models in-repo ([docs](https://grf-marl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tools/pretrain.html)) |
| **Real broadcast-grade rendering** | Cues carried in-band in an HLS stream (ID3/emsg timed metadata) and rendered by an actual broadcast graphics platform (Singular.live Data Streams, WebSocket, <300 ms, free tier) or the open-source CasparCG server with HTML templates. This is literally "in the way the platform's rendering partner handles overlays in the production path." | [Singular Data Stream API](https://developer.singular.live/data-stream-api/introduction); [timed metadata for interactive live streams](https://streamshark.io/blog/timed-event-metadata-the-foundation-for-interactive-live-streams/) |
| **Evidence of real-world impact** | A proper fan study (n ≈ 40) and an expert panel review, with results in the README. IBM's 20,000-fan survey says 56% want AI insight and commentary and 63% trust AI content, but *nobody has published a controlled comparison of explanation styles for football broadcast*. We can. | [IBM study, Aug 2025](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2025-08-18-ibm-study-sports-fans-demand-more-dynamic-digital-content,-powered-by-ai); [AO mute study, Jul 2026](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/07/08/study-half-of-uk-football-fans-mute-the-commentary/) |
| **A second submission** | Rules §4.5: an entrant may submit more than one project if each is "unique and substantially different." A developer-facing synthetic-data-and-evaluation platform is substantially different from a fan-facing broadcast product and targets a different category prize. | Official Rules §4.5, §8 (one Grand + one Category per *project*) |

The strategic frame stays the same as before: **treat the simulator as a physics engine you can branch, forecast and search**, not as a data source. What changes is that we can now have *two* worlds (a fast branchable one and a realistic rendered one), learned models that make the forecasts and explanations sharper, broadcast-real rendering, and measured human impact.

### II.2 Seven new ideas that only exist because of 45 days

#### Idea 8 — THE LIVING SEASON (story engine with memory)

**One line.** Not one match: a synthetic season with form, injuries, rivalries and a title race, so every match has history and every fan's experience carries memory across matches.

**Why it matters.** Football narratives are *serial*. "Verdant lost the reverse fixture 3–0 after a red card; tonight Diallo faces his old club" is what human broadcasters add that stats cannot. A season generator with an LLM-authored narrative bank makes personalisation deep: the system remembers what *you* watched, who *you* follow, and tells tonight's story in that light. It also hits the rules' "data innovation" bullet at a level nobody else will reach: a 20-club, 38-round synthetic league with event and tracking data for every match, plus storylines.

**Build.** Season simulator (fixtures, Elo-like form drift, injuries, fatigue carryover, transfer-window noise); narrative bank authored by an LLM from the season's actual results (so stories are grounded); a **Showrunner agent** that plans each match's narrative arcs per viewer (pre-match hooks, mid-match callbacks, post-match payoffs); viewer memory store.

**Risk.** Narrative quality can drift into melodrama (mitigate: Showrunner must cite season facts; Fact Checker applies). Feasibility: High (1 week, mostly engine + prompts).

#### Idea 9 — OPEN PITCH (synthetic data and evaluation platform; the second submission)

**One line.** A developer product: generate football-realistic synthetic leagues (events, tracking, pressure, metadata, storylines) through an API, an MCP server and a web studio; evaluate any football AI against hidden ground truth; publish a public benchmark.

**Why it matters.** The rules say a project may be "a data platform." Every one of the 433 entries wrote a throwaway generator; none built a reusable, validated one. Open Pitch is substantially different from WHYLINE (developer-facing, no fan experience) so it qualifies as a second submission, and it maps cleanly onto the **Best Use of Microsoft Foundry** category text: "accelerate development, orchestration and management," synthetic dataset generation, evaluations, Agent Optimizer.

**Build.** Reuse the Kickoff engine and the Living Season; add a generation API with "style dials" (press, tempo, directness, chaos), realism reports against public distributions, hidden-truth evaluation harness ("submit your momentum detector, get precision/recall"), a benchmark suite (`FootBench-Synth`: state detection, anticipation, pass difficulty, explanation groundedness), MCP server for agents, dataset cards, Foundry Evaluations integration, Fabric Eventhouse mirror for KQL exploration.

**Risk.** Judges may see overlap with WHYLINE (mitigate: separate repo, separate name, separate video, WHYLINE listed as a *consumer* of Open Pitch). Feasibility: High (it falls out of WHYLINE's own infrastructure; ~1 week of packaging).

#### Idea 10 — VIRTUAL STUDIO (auto-produced half-time and full-time shows)

**One line.** At the whistle, an AI studio show is produced automatically: a presenter (avatar or stylised 3D character) walks through the half's three sliding-doors moments with graphics, the Panel debates one, Dugout gives the manager's options, in the viewer's language.

**Why it matters.** "Automated match recaps suitable for studio" is in the challenge text. A text recap is table stakes; a *produced show* with a presenter, graphics and pacing is what a broadcaster actually ships. Real-time avatar APIs are now cheap (Anam, Tavus, HeyGen at roughly $0.10–0.40 per minute) and Google's Gemini Live Avatar is GA for enterprise; a stylised in-house 3D presenter avoids likeness and uncanny-valley issues entirely.

**Build.** Show Producer agent (rundown planning from the blackboard), graphics scene templates (Remotion or the R3F scene), presenter voice (ElevenLabs) and avatar (vendor API or own 3D), per-language output, HLS packaging. Optional "clip factory" for 15-second vertical cuts.

**Risk.** Avatar licensing terms for a public demo; pacing quality. Feasibility: Medium-High (1 week after the recap editor exists).

#### Idea 11 — BROADCAST-GRADE RENDER (the production path)

**One line.** Deliver cues the way broadcasters do: in-band timed metadata in an HLS stream of the rendered match, consumed by a real graphics platform (Singular.live Data Streams, CasparCG HTML templates, OBS browser source), with frame-accurate sync.

**Why it matters.** The challenge says outputs should be "timed and machine-readable enough to be rendered as synchronized graphical overlays on a live match feed, in the way the platform's rendering partner handles overlays in the production path." Everybody else pushes JSON to a React page. We can show the Cue Sheet driving *actual* broadcast graphics with measured sync error, and a drop-in adapter pattern for any partner.

**Build.** HLS packager (ffmpeg or a small Go service) that injects ID3/emsg from the Cue Sheet; hls.js consumer that fires overlay events on `FRAG_PARSING_METADATA`; Singular Data Stream adapter (PUT to private token endpoint); CasparCG HTML template that implements `update(json)`; OBS scene; a sync-error measurement harness (timestamp in frame vs cue time).

**Risk.** Singular free tier watermark and rate limits (fine for a demo; document). Feasibility: High (3–4 days).

#### Idea 12 — TWO WORLDS (fast engine + realistic engine, cross-validated)

**One line.** Run two simulators: Kickoff (our batched numpy engine, fast and branchable) and Google Research Football with DB-Football's pretrained 11v11 policies (realistic 3D, emergent play, rendered pixels). Calibrate the fast world to the realistic one and validate every explanation and forecast in both.

**Why it matters.** The weakest part of every synthetic-data entry is "your simulator defines the truth, so your results are circular." Two independent worlds break the circularity: a momentum detector or a counterfactual that holds in both has real evidential weight. gfootball also provides realistic *video* for Pitchwatch and Director's Cut without a single art asset.

**Build.** Sprint-1 spike: Docker install, headless steps-per-second, `get_state`/`set_state` branching cost, DB-Football policy loading, frame dumps at 720p. Then: an event extractor over gfootball observations (SoccER did this in 2020), a calibration script that fits Kickoff's decision parameters to gfootball's event distributions, and a cross-world evaluation report.

**Risk.** gfootball is archived and C++-heavy; the Python version pin may fight modern tooling (mitigate: Docker image pinned; it is a batch tool, never on the hot path). Feasibility: Medium (the spike decides by day 4).

#### Idea 13 — THE ACCESSIBLE MATCH

**One line.** Audio description for blind and low-vision fans, easy-language mode, high-contrast overlays, haptic patterns for goals and pressure on phones, captions in six languages, all generated from the same verified blackboard.

**Why it matters.** Impact that judges feel, and a personalisation dimension that is genuinely "made for every fan." Audio description must be spatial and timely ("ball on the right, 30 metres out, two defenders closing"), which our tracking data gives us directly. One competing entry gestures at it with template lines; a co-designed, tested version would stand alone.

**Build.** Describer agent constrained to spatial, verified language; priority rules (describe the ball, then threat, then context); Web Vibration API patterns; WCAG-checked overlay theme; recruit one or two blind football fans for two feedback sessions (RNIB and local supporters' groups are the route).

**Risk.** Recruiting testers in time. Feasibility: High (3–4 days).

#### Idea 14 — THE WORLD MODEL (moonshot)

**One line.** Distill the realistic engine into a learned transformer world model that rolls out thousands of futures per second on one GPU, replacing hand-written physics for forecasts and counterfactuals.

**Why it matters.** It is the research-grade version of the whole platform: the Oracle, Sliding Doors and Dugout all become GPU-batched inference. It is also the kind of thing that gets a hackathon entry written up afterwards.

**Build.** Train on ~5,000 gfootball matches of tracking (positions, velocities, actions), next-state prediction with uncertainty, autoregressive rollouts, fidelity checks against the engine. Heteroscedastic diffusion for multi-agent trajectories is current research ([arXiv 2605.10717](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.10717)).

**Risk.** High; could consume two weeks for a marginal demo gain. Only attempt if Sprints 1–3 finish early. Feasibility: Low-Medium.

```
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
L0  WORLDS        Kickoff (numpy, batched, branchable) · gfootball + DB-Football policies (3D, rendered, emergent)
                  Living Season (fixtures, form, injuries, storylines) · LLM-authored league · Event/tracking schemas (StatsBomb-like)
```

### III.1 Design decisions worth making explicit

1. **One rollout primitive.** `rollout(state, overrides, B, horizon) -> futures` is the single most important function in the codebase. Sliding Doors calls it with a past state and one alternative action; Oracle calls it with the current state every two seconds; Dugout calls it with style overrides. Optimise it once (numpy batch, then JAX if needed), test it exhaustively, publish its calibration.
2. **Deterministic first, LLM second.** Metrics, verification and scheduling are code. LLMs explain, narrate, debate and localise. Every LLM output is structured JSON with claims bound to evidence IDs, verified before it is ever rendered. This is what makes the system enterprise-credible and is the reason the Fact Checker is a separate party from the Narrator.
3. **Agents touch data only through MCP.** The newsroom cannot read Cosmos directly; it calls `whyline-mcp` tools. That makes the tool surface the audit boundary, lets GitHub Copilot and external agents use the same tools, and makes A2A exposure trivial.
4. **Two worlds, one schema.** gfootball observations are translated into the same event and tracking schema as Kickoff. Every evaluation runs on both. Explanations that only hold in one world are flagged.
5. **Cue Sheet is the product boundary.** Everything above L4 consumes the Cue Sheet; everything below produces it. A rendering partner integrates at that line and nowhere else.
6. **Replay mode is sacred.** The golden season (seed 4242) plays end to end without Azure, with recorded agent outputs, on any laptop. Judges can run it; CI runs it nightly.

## Part IV — Evaluation and evidence plan

This is where 45 days buys the most credibility. Every table below is published in the README with the scripts that produce it.

### IV.1 Technical evaluation

| What | Metric | Data | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| State detection (Settled/Transitional/Chaotic) vs hidden truth | precision, recall, F1 | 200 Kickoff matches + 100 gfootball matches | F1 ≥ 0.75 in both worlds |
| Counterfactual calibration | Brier score of P(goal in 90 s) | 5,000 branch points | ≤ 0.18 |
| Anticipation (learned model) | ADE/FDE at 4 s; AUC for P(shot in 10 s) | held-out 300 matches | FDE ≤ 6 m; AUC ≥ 0.80 |
| Agreement: learned forecaster vs simulator rollouts | correlation of P(shot) | live | reported, not targeted |
| Pass difficulty model | log-loss, calibration curve | held-out | reported |
| Vision (Pitchwatch) on rendered footage | detection mAP; tracking HOTA-style; event P/R by type | 2,000 labelled frames + 50 clips | pass P/R ≥ 0.85; shots ≥ 0.95 |
| Argon tagger vs detector pipeline | event P/R, latency, cost per clip | same clips | reported |
| Fact Checker | planted false claims caught; true claims wrongly rejected | 500 planted errors; golden set | 100%; ≤ 2% |
| Groundedness of rendered lines | Foundry groundedness evaluator | 1,000 cues, 6 languages | ≥ 0.95 |
| Multilingual fidelity | back-translation BLEU + native-speaker rating (5 languages, 2 raters each) | 200 lines per language | rating ≥ 4/5 |
| Latency | event → verified cue on Web PubSub | App Insights, 10 matches | p50 < 3 s, p95 < 5 s |
| Overlay sync error (HLS metadata) | ms between cue time and on-screen frame | 200 cues | ≤ 120 ms |
| Resilience | cues lost when an agent replica is killed mid-moment | chaos test, 20 runs | 0 (checkpoint resume) |
| Cost | per 90-minute match, 6 languages, all agents | Foundry + vendor usage | < $1.50 |

### IV.2 Human evaluation (the real-world impact evidence)

**Fan study.** n ≈ 40 (recruit via supporters' forums, university clubs, colleagues; incentive: a small voucher). Within-subject, four 6-minute passages of the golden match, each shown in one condition, order counterbalanced:

1. Plain broadcast (no overlays, template commentary)
2. Stats overlays (xG, possession, momentum) with evidence-only explanations
3. WHYLINE Sliding Doors explanations (counterfactual + evidence)
4. WHYLINE personalised (their mode, club, language, Unmute voice)

Measures per passage: a 5-question comprehension quiz ("why did the pressure rise at 31'?"), enjoyment and trust (7-point), "would you unmute this?" (yes/no), free text. Analysis: repeated-measures comparison; report effect sizes with confidence intervals; pre-register the hypotheses in the repo before data collection so judges see rigour.

**Expert panel.** Two or three analysts or coaches (local clubs, analytics community) rate 30 explanations and 10 Dugout recommendations for plausibility on a 5-point scale, blind to which world produced them. Report agreement.

**Accessibility sessions.** Two sessions with blind or low-vision fans on the Describer mode; iterate between sessions; report what changed.

## Part V — 45-day roadmap

Six sprints plus buffer. Team of three to four: **A** engines and ML, **B** agents and cloud, **C** front-end and broadcast, **D** (if available) evaluation, data, study, docs and video. With three people, D's work is shared and the World Model is dropped.

A submission-ready build is tagged at the end of Sprints 1, 3, 4 and 6 to cover any deadline.

### Sprint 1 — Days 1–7 (Oct 21–27): the 7-day product, plus two spikes

Deliver the full `WHYLINE_PROPOSAL.md` scope: Kickoff engine, pitch physics, Sliding Doors, newsroom with Fact Checker and Director, three front-ends, Azure deploy, evals v0, 2-minute video v1. **Tag `v0.1-submission`.**

Spikes (A, 2 days total, in parallel with the above):
- **gfootball spike:** Docker build, headless steps/s, `get_state`/`set_state` branch cost, DB-Football 11v11 policy load, 720p frame dumps. Decision gate day 4: adopt as World B, or drop and render footage from the R3F scene instead.
- **Rollout performance spike:** 200 × 60 s rollouts in < 800 ms on one vCPU with numpy; if not, JAX.

### Sprint 2 — Days 8–14: Two Worlds, the Oracle, broadcast render

- A: gfootball event extractor + schema translation; calibration of Kickoff to gfootball distributions; Oracle forecast loop (2 s cadence) from rollouts; start anticipation-model training data generation (2,000 Kickoff matches, 500 gfootball matches).
- B: Dugout parameter search as an MCP tool; Panel (three pundits + chair, group-chat orchestration, mandatory tool citations); A2A agent cards for each agent; AG-UI streaming to Studio.
- C: HLS packager with in-band cues; hls.js consumer; Singular.live Data Stream adapter; CasparCG HTML template; OBS scene; sync-error harness.
- D: evaluation harness v1 across both worlds; recruit study participants; pre-register the fan study.

### Sprint 3 — Days 15–21: Unmute, Director's Cut, learned models

- A: train anticipation transformer; phase classifier; publish ADE/FDE/AUC; wire the learned forecaster beside rollouts in the Oracle overlay.
- B: Unmute voice stack (ElevenLabs v4 Turbo personas, pacing engine, ducking), Gemini 3.8 Live barge-in Q&A with memory, Describer agent for audio description, Content Safety on all audio text.
- C: Director's Cut: R3F 3D broadcast scene, camera rigs (wide, tactical, follow, behind-goal, replay dolly), per-viewer Director decisions, replay system, per-viewer HLS output.
- D: run fan study pilot (n = 6), fix instruments; accessibility session 1. **Tag `v0.3-submission`** with video v2.

### Sprint 4 — Days 22–28: Living Season, Pitchwatch, Virtual Studio

- A: Living Season generator (20 clubs, 38 rounds, form, injuries, fatigue carryover); LLM-authored narrative bank grounded in results; Pitchwatch vision: render 2,000 labelled frames, fine-tune RF-DETR/YOLO26, pitch keypoints + homography, ByteTrack, event extractor; Argon tagger comparison; accuracy table.
- B: Showrunner agent with viewer memory; Show Producer agent for the Virtual Studio rundown; Routines for scheduled post-match reports.
- C: Virtual Studio show (presenter, graphics scenes, per-language), Clip Factory (Remotion vertical cuts), Fan App season view, Player-Cam season stats.
- D: run the main fan study (n ≈ 40); expert panel review; multilingual rater sessions. **Tag `v0.4-submission`** (covers a 21 Nov deadline with margin).

### Sprint 5 — Days 29–35: Open Pitch (second submission) and enterprise hardening

- A+D: Open Pitch packaging: generation API with style dials, realism reports, hidden-truth evaluation service, `FootBench-Synth` benchmark, dataset cards, separate public repo, Foundry Evaluations and Fabric Eventhouse integration, its own 2-minute video.
- B: enterprise hardening of WHYLINE: multi-tenant config, RBAC, Key Vault, managed identity audit, per-cue audit trail export, cost guard, SLO dashboards (Monitor workbooks), Azure Load Testing run (500 concurrent fan clients), chaos test (kill each agent type), Responsible AI notes (club-bias guard, no betting language, content safety), threat model, data-residency notes.
- C: polish pass on all surfaces; accessibility session 2; WCAG audit; RTL layouts.

### Sprint 6 — Days 36–42: evidence, story, submission

- Write up all evaluation tables and the fan study results; architecture docs with exported workflow visualisations; `docs/copilot-log.md` showing Copilot custom agents and Azure MCP Server use during the build; README that reads like a product page with a 90-second "run it yourself" path.
- Final 2-minute video (storyboard in Part VII), screenshots, submission text for both projects.
- Freeze `main`; keep the hosted demo alive with scale-to-zero through the judging period. **Tag `v1.0-submission`.**

### Buffer — Days 43–45

Bug bash on a clean machine, re-record anything broken, submit both projects, keep a day in hand. If everything is green by day 40, the World Model moonshot gets the spare days, never the buffer.

## Part VIII — What to decide now

1. **Confirm the real deadline** on the Projects page. It decides whether Sprint 1 is the whole game or the opening.
2. **Team size and the 3D question.** Director's Cut and Virtual Studio need one person comfortable with React-Three-Fiber for three weeks. Without that person, World B's rendered frames (if the gfootball spike passes) become the "video," and Director's Cut shrinks to replay and overlay decisions on that footage.
3. **Two submissions or one.** Open Pitch costs about a week and doubles the category-prize surface. I recommend yes if there are four people, and no with three.
4. **Model providers.** Pick one frontier model for reasoning (Claude Opus 5.5 or Gemini 4 Argon) and one cheap model for volume, both behind the Agent Framework adapter. Everything else in the stack is already provider-agnostic.

The next concrete step is unchanged whatever you decide: scaffold the repo and build the Kickoff engine, because every one of the fourteen ideas stands on it.
